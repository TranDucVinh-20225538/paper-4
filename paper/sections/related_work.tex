\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Distance-Based Reliability Estimation}

Distance-based out-of-distribution detection scores an input by its position
relative to the training distribution in representation space. The
Mahalanobis detector fits class-conditional Gaussians to training features
and scores an input by its minimum Mahalanobis distance to any class
centroid~\cite{lee2018simple}; nearest-neighbour, kernel-density, and
cosine-to-centroid variants replace the parametric fit with local or
directional statistics but retain the same premise, that distributional
novelty manifests as geometric displacement. The family is well suited to
clinical deployment because it requires no outlier data at training time and
attaches to an existing classifier without retraining~\cite{lee2018simple}.
The premise is nonetheless an assumption about representation geometry, and
this paper concerns the conditions under which it holds.

\subsection{Decodability Versus Accessibility}

Linear probing trains a classifier independently of the model in order to
measure what information a representation linearly
encodes~\cite{alain2016probes}. The distinction matters here because a
probe and a distance-based estimator can disagree: a probe may recover
acquisition-domain identity from a representation on which distance-based
scores are uninformative. Prior work on convolutional networks reported exactly this dissociation for
skin-lesion representations: probes recovered acquisition-domain identity at
0.72--0.81 AUROC while Mahalanobis-family scores sat near 0.40 under the
standard higher-distance-is-more-OOD convention~\cite{tran2026decodable}.
That report is explicit that the scores did not approach chance but were
directionally misaligned: their orientation-free separability was 0.58--0.60,
so the ordering retained signal while pointing the wrong way. That report motivates the
present evaluation; whether it describes the frozen foundation-model regime
is the question we examine.

\subsection{Representation Geometry and Estimator Performance}

Estimator performance has been linked to two properties of the
in-distribution representation: its local intrinsic dimensionality, estimated
by the Levina--Bickel maximum-likelihood estimator~\cite{levina2004maximum},
and the decay profile of its within-class covariance
spectrum~\cite{yu2510dissecting}. That work evaluates a range of frozen
vision foundation-model backbones and reports that these two quantities track
how Mahalanobis-style detector performance varies across them. The
relationship is characterized as tracking rather than causation, and the
study does not employ probing classifiers or examine the dissociation
described above. Its interventions target an internal term of its own
derivation rather than detector performance itself.

A substantial body of work manipulates related geometric quantities directly,
mostly outside out-of-distribution detection. Post-hoc whitening and
isotropy-oriented transforms reshape the covariance spectrum of frozen
embeddings, either by removing dominant principal
directions~\cite{mu2018allbutthetop}, by full or partial
decorrelation~\cite{su2021whitening,diera2024isotropy}, or by graded
per-dimension spectral reweighting~\cite{li2603spectral}. Evidence on
semantic cost is mixed: partial-strength variants report downstream gains,
while a systematic evaluation of five full-strength whitening variants
reports degradation on classification tasks~\cite{forooghi2024whitening}.
A separate line targets geometry during training rather than post
hoc, through spectral or intrinsic-dimensionality
regularizers~\cite{weng2023modulate,huang2024ldreg}. We draw the
geometry-manipulation operator used in our causal comparison from the
post-hoc, dose-controllable end of this literature, for reasons detailed in
Section~\ref{sec:methods}.

\subsection{Frozen Foundation Models and Cross-Backbone Comparison}

Dermatological image analysis has moved toward large pretrained encoders used
as frozen feature extractors, with lightweight task-specific components
trained on top~\cite{yan2025panderm}. In frozen foundation models, the
representation geometry is primarily inherited from large-scale pretraining
rather than optimized end-to-end for the downstream clinical task. Comparing
geometry and detector performance across such backbones establishes that the
two covary, but backbones differ simultaneously in pretraining corpus,
objective, architecture, and scale, so an association measured across them
cannot separate a geometric explanation from a shared dependence on
pretraining. Distinguishing these readings requires holding provenance fixed
and intervening on geometry directly. Our contribution is an evaluation of
that kind, in the same spirit as validation studies that test whether a
reported effect survives a change of setting, applied here to the
explanatory status of a reported association rather than to its
reproducibility.
