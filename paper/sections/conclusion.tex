\section{Conclusion}

We tested whether an association reported across representations, between
in-distribution geometry and distance-based estimator performance, can
account for the previously reported probe--reliability dissociation when
geometry is intervened upon within a single frozen dermatology foundation
model. Under a preregistered protocol incorporating implementation-integrity
verification and manipulation checking, that explanatory reading received
limited empirical support in the evaluated configuration. The dissociation was
reproduced in direction and with high statistical confidence, but at a
magnitude approximately two orders of magnitude smaller than originally
reported, and neither preregistered geometry metric showed a Holm-corrected
association with estimator performance.

These findings indicate that explanatory accounts established in one
architectural regime warrant direct evaluation before being carried into
another. The protocol reported here---gating on implementation integrity,
confirming that the manipulated quantity moves before any causal comparison
is interpreted, and fixing the multiplicity correction in advance of
execution---is specific neither to this account nor to this backbone, and
provides a basis for assessing whether a previously reported effect, and the
explanation advanced for it, hold under changed conditions.
