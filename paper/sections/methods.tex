\section{Methods}
\label{sec:methods}

\subsection{Backbone and Populations}

All experiments use a single frozen pretrained dermatology foundation model,
PanDerm ViT-L/16~\cite{yan2025panderm}, as a feature extractor. The backbone is never
updated: every intervention described below operates on its output
embeddings, which are $1024$-dimensional. Embeddings were extracted once
under a fixed preprocessing pipeline and stored as an immutable artifact with
checksum verification, so that all downstream analyses share bit-identical
inputs.

The in-distribution population is ISIC 2019~\cite{tschandl2018ham10000,%
hernandezperez2024bcn20000,codella2018isbi}, comprising $25{,}331$
dermoscopic images across eight diagnostic classes. We partition it with a
fixed seed into a held-out evaluation set ($20\%$, $n = 5{,}067$), a
validation set ($20\%$ of the remainder, $n = 4{,}053$), and a training
population ($n = 16{,}211$) used to fit all distribution-dependent
quantities. The validation split is not used in the analyses reported here.
This partition is specific to the present study and does not correspond to
the challenge's own splits.

The domain-shifted population is PAD-UFES-20~\cite{pacheco2020padufes},
contributing $2{,}298$ close-up clinical images collected in Brazil; the
modality difference from ISIC's dermoscopic images accompanies differences in
device, population, and clinical setting. PAD-UFES-20 is mapped into the same
eight-class label space and populates six of the eight classes. The
evaluation population combines held-out ISIC and PAD-UFES images, $n =
7{,}365$ in total. Class labels are used for the in-distribution task and for
class-conditional estimator fitting; the domain label (ISIC versus PAD-UFES)
is the target of the domain probe.

Neither ISIC 2019 nor PAD-UFES-20 forms part of the backbone's pretraining
corpus, which draws on eleven separately described
sources~\cite{yan2025panderm}.

We additionally define a fixed nuisance direction
$w = \mathrm{unit}(\mu_{\mathrm{ISIC}} - \mu_{\mathrm{PAD}})$, the unit vector
between population means, computed once and frozen before any intervention
was trained.

\subsection{Geometry Metrics}

Four geometric quantities are measured on every evaluated representation. Two
are preregistered as the metrics the account under evaluation specifies; two
are recorded alongside them.

\emph{Local intrinsic dimensionality} (LID) is estimated per point by the
Levina--Bickel maximum-likelihood estimator~\cite{levina2004maximum} with
$k = 50$ neighbours,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{LID}_k(z) = -\left[\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}
    \log \frac{r_j(z)}{r_k(z)}\right]^{-1},
\end{equation}
where $r_j(z)$ is the distance from $z$ to its $j$-th nearest neighbour in the
same feature set, and reported as the dataset mean.

\emph{Within-class spectral-decay slope} is obtained from the within-class
scatter matrix $S_w = \frac{1}{N}\sum_c \sum_{i: y_i = c} (z_i - \mu_c)(z_i -
\mu_c)^{\top}$, computed without regularization so that tail eigenvalues are
not distorted. Its eigenvalues are sorted in descending order and fitted by
ordinary least squares as $\log \lambda_i = a + b\,i$ over the full range
$i = 1,\dots,d$; the reported slope is $|b|$.

\emph{Condition number} is computed on the fitted Mahalanobis precision
matrix, and \emph{per-direction variance} is the variance of the projection
of the evaluation embeddings onto the frozen nuisance direction,
$\mathrm{Var}(z^{\top} w)$.

\subsection{Representation Intervention and Dose Ladder}
\label{sec:intervention}

The intervention is a bottleneck adapter applied to the frozen embedding,
\begin{equation}
  z' = z + W_2\,\mathrm{GELU}(W_1 z),
\end{equation}
with bottleneck width $r = 16$ and the up-projection $W_2$ initialized to
zero, so that the adapter is exactly the identity at initialization. The
Intervention arm is trained with a task loss on the eight-class diagnostic
objective plus an orthogonality penalty against the frozen nuisance direction,
weighted by $\lambda_{\mathrm{proj}} = 0.1$. Training uses AdamW, learning
rate $10^{-3}$, batch size $512$, for $100$ epochs, repeated across five
seeds $\{42, 52, 62, 72, 82\}$. Bottleneck width and $\lambda_{\mathrm{proj}}$
were selected by a preregistered rule fixed before the canonical runs.

Intervention strength is varied post hoc rather than by retraining. For each
trained adapter we compute its displacement $\Delta z = W_2\,\mathrm{GELU}(W_1
z)$ and evaluate the ladder
\begin{equation}
  z'(\alpha) = z + \alpha\,\Delta z, \qquad
  \alpha \in \{0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0\},
\end{equation}
so that $\alpha = 0$ recovers the unmodified representation and $\alpha = 1$
the fully trained adapter. This isolates strength from optimization variance:
every rung of a given seed's ladder derives from one optimization run.

\subsection{Control Arms}

Two controls accompany the Intervention arm. The \emph{Conventional} arm is
trained identically---same adapter, same width, same schedule, same
seeds---but with the task loss alone and no orthogonality penalty, giving a
matched-budget ordinary fine-tuning comparison that separates intervention
\emph{type} from intervention \emph{budget}. The \emph{Adaptation} arm spans
three capacity rungs (linear probe with the embedding unchanged; partial
fine-tuning at $r = 8$; full adapter fine-tuning at $r = 16$), all trained
with the task loss alone, and supplies the additional configurations required
by the correlational analysis.

\subsection{Verification Gates}

No checkpoint contributes to a reported comparison before clearing two gates
specified in advance.

\emph{Gate 0} (implementation integrity) confirms that the intervention left
the representation usable and the domain signal present. It requires domain
probe accuracy above the majority-class baseline by a fixed margin
($> 0.688 + 0.05$) and in-distribution eight-class balanced accuracy above
chance by a fixed margin ($> 0.125 + 0.10$). Both probes are
$\ell_2$-regularized logistic regressions on standardized features, averaged
over five probe seeds. Thresholds were fixed before execution. A Gate 0
failure means the intervention destroyed either the signal being studied or
the task itself, and is reported as inconclusive for that checkpoint rather
than as evidence.

\emph{Gate 1} (manipulation check) confirms that the intervention actually
moved the geometry the account concerns. It is satisfied when at least one
preregistered geometry metric shows reproducible dose dependence across the
ladder; the criterion makes no assumption of linearity, strict monotonicity,
or convexity, and the remaining metrics are recorded rather than discarded.
An unmanipulated checkpoint tests nothing, so Gate 1 failures are excluded
from causal interpretation rather than counted as negative evidence.

\subsection{Reliability Estimators}
\label{sec:estimators}

Four distance-based estimators are evaluated on every checkpoint. The
Mahalanobis detector fits class-conditional means and a shared precision
matrix on the adapted training population, with regularization
$\varepsilon = 10^{-5}$, and scores an input by its minimum squared
Mahalanobis distance to any class centroid~\cite{lee2018simple}. The cosine
estimator scores by maximum cosine similarity to a class centroid; the
nearest-neighbour estimator uses $k = 10$; the kernel-density estimator scores
by class-conditional density. All four are fitted on the adapted
\emph{training} population and evaluated on the held-out population.
Performance is reported as AUROC for separating in-distribution from
domain-shifted inputs, with FPR at 95\% TPR recorded alongside.

These four estimators are the family the hypothesis under evaluation concerns,
all of them scoring an input by its position relative to the training
distribution in representation space. Logit- and energy-based detectors are
not evaluated: they depend on classifier outputs rather than on representation
geometry alone, and so fall outside the hypothesis being tested. This study
does not aim to rank detectors.

\subsection{Geometry Manipulation for the Causal Comparison}

Testing whether geometry, rather than nominal intervention strength, tracks
estimator degradation requires an operator that moves the preregistered
geometry metrics directly. The normalization-based knob originally specified
for this role was found, on measurement, to move condition number
substantially while leaving LID and spectral-decay slope unchanged. This is
consistent with the operator's design, which adjusts feature radii while
holding directions fixed~\cite{yu2510dissecting}; both preregistered metrics
are invariant to such rescaling. It was therefore not used.

The operator adopted instead is a fixed-exponent spectral
tempering~\cite{li2603spectral}. Given the eigendecomposition of the
in-distribution training covariance with eigenvalues $\lambda_i$ and
eigenvectors $U$, embeddings are transformed as
\begin{equation}
  z' = \mu + U \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\lambda_i^{-\gamma/2}\right)
       U^{\top} (z - \mu),
\label{eq:tempering}
  \end{equation}
where $\gamma = 0$ is the identity and $\gamma = 1$ is full whitening. We use
a fixed, manually specified $\gamma$ rather than the adaptive per-dimension
exponent of the original formulation: adaptivity was introduced there to
avoid per-task tuning where no validation signal is available, whereas the
present protocol supplies an independent, preregistered criterion (Gate 1)
against which a prespecified $\gamma$ ladder is evaluated. This operator was
subjected to the same manipulation check before use, and its effect on LID
and spectral-decay slope is reported in Section~\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Scorer-Relevant Geometry: What the Operator Can and Cannot Move}
\label{sec:invariance}

A geometry metric is interpretable as a predictor of a scorer's ranking only if
the operator that moves the metric also moves the structure the scorer reads.
The two are routinely conflated: a metric is computed on a representation, an
AUROC is computed from a scorer applied to the same representation, and their
covariation across conditions is read as evidence that the geometry explains the
ranking. That inference requires a step that is rarely checked. This subsection
supplies it for the operator of \eqref{eq:tempering}, and the answer separates
the estimators of Section~\ref{sec:estimators} into two classes.

Write $\Sigma = U \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i) U^{\top}$ for the
in-distribution training covariance and
$A_{\gamma} = U \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i^{-\gamma/2}) U^{\top}$ for the
tempering map, so that \eqref{eq:tempering} reads
$z' = \mu + A_{\gamma}(z - \mu)$. Note that $A_{\gamma}$ is symmetric and, for
$\lambda_i > 0$, invertible.

\begin{proposition}[Condition number is a designed target]
\label{prop:kappa}
Under \eqref{eq:tempering} the transformed covariance is
$\Sigma' = U \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i^{1-\gamma}) U^{\top}$, and therefore
\begin{equation}
  \kappa(\Sigma') = \kappa(\Sigma)^{\,1-\gamma},
  \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
  \log \kappa(\Sigma') = (1-\gamma) \log \kappa(\Sigma).
  \label{eq:kappa-law}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
$\Sigma' = A_{\gamma} \Sigma A_{\gamma}^{\top}
 = U \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i^{-\gamma/2}) \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i)
     \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i^{-\gamma/2}) U^{\top}
 = U \operatorname{diag}(\lambda_i^{1-\gamma}) U^{\top}$.
The eigenvalues of $\Sigma'$ are $\lambda_i^{1-\gamma}$, and
$x \mapsto x^{1-\gamma}$ is increasing for $\gamma < 1$, so extremal eigenvalues
map to extremal eigenvalues and
$\kappa(\Sigma') = (\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min})^{1-\gamma}$.
\end{IEEEproof}

The dose ladder is thus not merely correlated with the condition number; it
sets it, on a log scale, by construction. $\gamma = 0$ leaves $\kappa$
unchanged and $\gamma = 1$ drives it to unity. This is the strongest position a
manipulation check can be in, and it is what makes the next result consequential
rather than a technicality.

\begin{proposition}[Mahalanobis scoring is invariant to the operator]
\label{prop:maha}
Let the Mahalanobis parameters be refit on the tempered training population, as
specified in Section~\ref{sec:estimators}. With ridge $\varepsilon = 0$, the
squared Mahalanobis score is preserved pointwise,
$d'^2(z') = d^2(z)$ for every $z$, and the AUROC of that score is therefore
identical for every $\gamma$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{IEEEproof}
Tempering is applied to every embedding, so class means map affinely,
$\mu_c' = \mu + A_{\gamma}(\mu_c - \mu)$, and the pooled within-class scatter
maps as $S' = A_{\gamma} S A_{\gamma}^{\top}$. With $\varepsilon = 0$ the refit
precision is $P' = A_{\gamma}^{-\top} P A_{\gamma}^{-1}$, while residuals map as
$z' - \mu_c' = A_{\gamma}(z - \mu_c)$. Hence
\begin{align}
  d'^2(z') &= (z' - \mu_c')^{\top} P' (z' - \mu_c') \nonumber\\
           &= (z - \mu_c)^{\top} A_{\gamma}^{\top} A_{\gamma}^{-\top}
              P A_{\gamma}^{-1} A_{\gamma} (z - \mu_c) \nonumber\\
           &= (z - \mu_c)^{\top} P (z - \mu_c) = d^2(z).
\end{align}
Minimisation over $c$ preserves the identity, and AUROC depends on the scores
only through their ordering.
\end{IEEEproof}

Nothing in the proof is specific to tempering: it holds for any invertible
linear map whose parameters are re-estimated afterwards. Tempering is simply
the member of that family this protocol uses.

Propositions~\ref{prop:kappa} and~\ref{prop:maha} bound one comparison in this
study, and it is important to be exact about which. They apply to the tempering
operator of \eqref{eq:tempering}, which is used only for the geometry-matched
comparison. They do \emph{not} apply to the dose ladder of
Section~\ref{sec:intervention}: the adapter there is
$z' = z + W_2\,\mathrm{GELU}(W_1 z)$, which is not affine, so no invariance
follows and the condition-number association reported across $\alpha$ stands on
its own footing.

For the geometry-matched comparison the consequence is sharp. Condition number
is set by design through \eqref{eq:kappa-law} while the Mahalanobis ranking
cannot move at all, so for that estimator the comparison had no difference
available to detect. Its null is entailed by the algebra rather than measured,
and it is neither evidence of a small effect nor a shortfall of power at five
seeds. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports it as a bound on what that comparison
can speak to. The same comparison remains informative for the three estimators
below, which are not invariant.

The remaining estimators are not affine-invariant and are not covered by
Proposition~\ref{prop:maha}. Cosine similarity is computed after
$\ell_2$ normalisation and so depends on directions that $A_{\gamma}$ rotates
relative to the sphere; nearest-neighbour and kernel-density scores depend on
Euclidean neighbourhood structure, which $A_{\gamma}$ rescales anisotropically.
For these three, condition number remains admissible as a candidate predictor,
and the association reported for them is interpretable in a way the Mahalanobis
association is not. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports a sweep confirming this
separation on the reference embeddings: across $\gamma \in [0, 1]$ the realized
condition number moves eight orders of magnitude while Mahalanobis AUROC moves
$1.3 \times 10^{-5}$, against $1.5 \times 10^{-2}$ for cosine and
$1.6 \times 10^{-3}$ for $k$-NN.

Two limits of this analysis are worth stating plainly. First,
\eqref{eq:kappa-law} is an identity in exact arithmetic. The smallest sample
eigenvalue in these embeddings is of order $10^{-17}$, so $\lambda_i^{-\gamma/2}$
on the tail of the spectrum is dominated by round-off and the recomputed
condition number departs from $\kappa(\Sigma)^{1-\gamma}$ while remaining
monotone in $\gamma$. We report the identity as algebra and the realized values
as measurement, and do not treat them as the same quantity. Second, the ridge
$\varepsilon = 10^{-5}$ used in practice breaks exact invariance, since
$\varepsilon I$ does not transform with $A_{\gamma}$; the observed
$1.3 \times 10^{-5}$ drift in Mahalanobis AUROC is consistent with that residual
and with floating-point error, and no claim of exact numerical constancy is
made.

We do not attempt a corresponding result for local intrinsic dimensionality.
LID is a property of local neighbourhood structure rather than of the global
covariance spectrum, and the empirical profile reported in
Section~\ref{sec:results} is non-monotonic in $\gamma$ and consistent across all
five seeds. A closed-form statement would be weaker than that observation and
would risk presenting an assumption as a derivation. It is reported as an
empirical finding and left there.

\subsection{Preregistered Claims and Statistical Analysis}

Claims, tests, and multiplicity correction were fixed before execution. We
say the account is supported by \emph{mediation} if the association between
intervention and estimator degradation is carried by the geometry metrics
rather than by intervention type or nominal strength.

To evaluate the correlational claim, we compute Kendall's $\tau$ between each
geometry metric and each estimator's AUROC, pooling Intervention-arm
strengths and Adaptation-arm rungs. Because configurations from a common seed
share an optimization run, these pooled points are not fully independent, and
the resulting $\tau$ values are interpreted as descriptive associations across
the configuration set rather than as estimates from independent draws. The
control-arm analysis below models the seed dependence explicitly, and a post-hoc
sensitivity analysis reported with the results quantifies what the correlational
$p$-values become when the seed, rather than the configuration, is taken as the
unit of resampling. To evaluate the causal claim, we compare
estimator degradation between a geometry-matched pair and a
nominal-strength-matched pair, using a paired $t$-test with a Wilcoxon
signed-rank test reported alongside, normality being assessed at analysis
time rather than assumed. To evaluate the control-arm claim, we fit a
mixed-effects model with an intervention-type-by-geometry interaction and
seed as grouping factor. To evaluate probe decodability, we test probe AUROC
against chance and against the corresponding Mahalanobis AUROC across seeds.
Holm--Bonferroni correction is applied across the estimator-by-metric
comparison families, and both corrected and uncorrected $p$-values are
reported.
