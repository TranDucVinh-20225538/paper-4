\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Gate Outcomes}

All Intervention-arm and Conventional-arm checkpoints cleared Gate 0 at every
rung of the dose ladder, confirming that domain information and
in-distribution task performance survived the intervention. Gate 1 was
satisfied on the Intervention arm: at least one preregistered geometry metric
showed reproducible dose dependence across the ladder and across all five
seeds. No checkpoint was excluded from the analyses below on gate grounds.

\subsection{Probe Decodability and the Distance-Based Estimators}

Across all ten evaluated configurations, a linear probe recovered
acquisition-domain identity essentially perfectly, with mean probe AUROC
ranging from $0.99999$ to $1.00000$. Mahalanobis AUROC on the same
representations ranged from $0.9983$ to $0.9984$. Probe AUROC exceeded chance
in every configuration ($p < 10^{-19}$ throughout), and exceeded the
corresponding Mahalanobis AUROC in every configuration where the paired test
was defined, with $p$ between $2.5\times10^{-5}$ and $8.4\times10^{-8}$ across
nine configurations; the remaining configuration (Adaptation, linear-probe
rung) produced zero variance across seeds and is reported as degenerate
rather than assigned a $t$-statistic.

The direction of the previously reported dissociation was therefore
reproduced, and reproduced with high statistical confidence. Its magnitude
was not. The mean probe-minus-Mahalanobis AUROC gap was
$1.6\times10^{-3}$ (range $1.567\times10^{-3}$ to $1.666\times10^{-3}$ across
configurations). The comparison figure in the convolutional setting depends on
a convention that source makes explicit, so we state both. Under the directed
higher-distance-is-more-OOD convention its linear probes stand $0.333$ above
Mahalanobis, a ratio of roughly $200$ against the present gap. Read
orientation-free, as $\max(\mathrm{AUROC}, 1-\mathrm{AUROC})$, the same
comparison gives $0.137$ and a ratio of roughly $86$, because a directed AUROC
of $0.402$ there reflects an inverted ordering rather than an absent
signal~\cite{tran2026decodable}.

The two settings are therefore not measuring one quantity, and the ratio should
not be read as though they were. Here both quantities sit near ceiling and the
gap is compressed against it; there, the gap is dominated by a sign inversion
in the estimator. What survives the difference in convention is the direction
and the order of magnitude: the separation between what a probe recovers and
what a distance-based estimator delivers is roughly two orders of magnitude
smaller in this regime under either reading ($10^{2.3}$ directed,
$10^{1.9}$ orientation-free). Distance-based estimators were already operating near
ceiling on the unmodified representation, with Reference-arm AUROC of
$0.9984$ (Mahalanobis), $0.9979$ (nearest-neighbour), $0.9805$ (cosine), and
$0.8942$ (kernel density).

\subsection{Geometry--Estimator Associations}

Table~\ref{tab:c2} reports Kendall's $\tau$ between each geometry metric and
each estimator's AUROC across the $30$ pooled configurations. Neither
preregistered geometry metric reached Holm-corrected significance against any
estimator: LID's strongest association was with Mahalanobis
($\tau = -0.24$, uncorrected $p = 0.065$), and spectral-decay slope's was
also with Mahalanobis ($\tau = 0.22$, uncorrected $p = 0.088$). Condition
number was associated with all four estimators at Holm-corrected significance
($\tau$ between $0.42$ and $0.58$, uncorrected $p$ between
$1.1\times10^{-5}$ and $1.3\times10^{-3}$). Per-direction variance along the
nuisance direction reached uncorrected significance against three estimators
($p$ between $0.009$ and $0.014$) but did not survive correction.

\subsubsection*{Sensitivity to the unit of resampling}

The Methods note that configurations drawn from a common seed share an
optimization run, so the $30$ pooled points are not fully independent. To put a
number on that caveat rather than leaving it qualitative, we repeated the
condition-number analysis with an exact permutation test that permutes
\emph{whole seeds} rather than individual configurations, preserving the
within-seed structure. This is a post-hoc sensitivity analysis and was not
preregistered.

The $\tau$ values are unchanged by construction; only the reference distribution
differs. Under seed-level permutation the uncorrected $p$-values are $0.133$
(Mahalanobis), $0.075$ (cosine), $0.108$ ($k$NN) and $0.025$ (kernel density).
None survives Holm correction across the four-estimator family, whose first
threshold is $0.0125$.

Two things follow, and they pull in opposite directions. The Holm-corrected
significance reported above rests on treating the pooled configurations as
independent draws, which the Methods already declined to claim; a reader who
takes the seed as the resampling unit should not read Table~\ref{tab:c2} as
establishing significance. But the permutation test is also very weak here: with
five seeds its reference distribution admits only $5! = 120$ arrangements, so its
$p$-values are quantised at multiples of $1/120 \approx 0.008$ and it has little
power to resolve an association of any size. These $p$-values therefore record a
failure to resolve, not a demonstration of absence, and we report both analyses
rather than choosing between them.

In the companion analysis, probe accuracy was associated with LID
($\tau = 0.39$, $p = 0.005$) and with per-direction variance
($\tau = 0.48$, $p = 5.4\times10^{-4}$), and was not associated with
spectral-decay slope ($\tau = -0.03$, $p = 0.85$) or with condition number
($\tau = -0.04$, $p = 0.77$).

\subsection{Intervention Type and Geometry}

All sixteen mixed-effects models converged. Three interaction terms reached
Holm-corrected significance: LID with Mahalanobis
($p = 2.9\times10^{-6}$), LID with kernel density ($p = 2.6\times10^{-3}$),
and condition number with Mahalanobis ($p = 3.9\times10^{-5}$). The
remaining thirteen did not, including all four slope interactions and all
four per-direction-variance interactions. Interaction coefficients for the
Holm-significant terms were small in absolute magnitude
($-1.2\times10^{-4}$, $-4.3\times10^{-2}$, and $2.9\times10^{-4}$
respectively).

\subsection{Geometry Manipulation and the Causal Comparison}

The normalization-based operator originally specified for the causal
comparison was measured across all twenty Intervention-arm checkpoints before
use. It moved condition number substantially (mean change $-2.7\times10^{5}$)
while leaving both preregistered metrics effectively unchanged: mean LID
change $-4\times10^{-4}$ (SD $1.8\times10^{-3}$) and mean slope change below
$10^{-6}$ (SD $10^{-6}$). It was therefore not used.

The adopted fixed-exponent spectral tempering operator was subjected to the
same check. Across five seeds it moved spectral-decay slope reproducibly and
near-monotonically ($-1.5\times10^{-3}$ at $\gamma = 0.25$, $-2.7\times10^{-3}$
at $\gamma = 0.5$, $-3.6\times10^{-3}$ at $\gamma = 0.75$, $-3.5\times10^{-3}$
at $\gamma = 1.0$), and moved LID reproducibly but non-monotonically, rising
by $+2.7$ to $+5.8$ at $\gamma \in \{0.25, 0.5\}$ before falling by $-1.5$ to
$-4.4$ at $\gamma = 1.0$; the same inverted profile appeared in all five
seeds. Gate 0 passed in all twenty post-transform configurations.

For the causal comparison, $\gamma$ was calibrated on a single seed to bring
the full-strength checkpoint's spectral-decay slope to the level occurring
naturally at the weakest intervention strength, giving $\gamma = 0.01$
(slope $6.746\times10^{-3}$ against a target of $6.751\times10^{-3}$). A
single calibrated value rather than a ladder is used here because the
comparison requires one specific condition---geometry matched to a reference
checkpoint while nominal strength differs---rather than a dose--response
relationship; the operator's dose behaviour is characterized separately by
the manipulation check reported above. At this
$\gamma$, LID remained $0.60$ below the matched checkpoint's value: the
comparison is therefore matched on spectral-decay slope, not on both
preregistered metrics jointly. Three configurations were then evaluated
across five seeds: full strength unmanipulated ($\alpha = 1.0$,
$\gamma = 0$), weak strength unmanipulated ($\alpha = 0.25$, $\gamma = 0$),
and full strength slope-matched to the weak checkpoint ($\alpha = 1.0$,
$\gamma = 0.01$). Gate 0 passed in all five manipulated configurations.

Table~\ref{tab:c3} reports estimator AUROC for the three configurations.
Mahalanobis and nearest-neighbour showed no separation between any pair of
configurations. Cosine and kernel density showed a separation between the
two unmanipulated strengths ($0.025$ and $0.110$ AUROC respectively), and on
both, configuration C was numerically closer to its nominal-strength
counterpart (A) than to its slope-matched counterpart (B).

None of the eight paired comparisons reached Holm-corrected significance. The
smallest corrected $p$-value was $0.111$ (nearest-neighbour,
geometry-matched), and the smallest uncorrected $p$-values were $0.014$
(nearest-neighbour, geometry-matched), $0.023$ (cosine,
nominal-strength-matched), and $0.048$ (kernel density, geometry-matched);
Wilcoxon signed-rank $p$-values were no smaller than $0.063$ throughout. With
five seeds and an eight-comparison correction family, statistical power was
limited, and the comparison is therefore reported as inconclusive at the
significance-test level.
