Distance-based estimators are widely used to flag domain-shifted inputs
before a clinical model acts on them. Two observations motivate this study.
On convolutional representations, a linear probe recovered acquisition-domain
identity while distance-based estimators on the same representations
performed near chance---a dissociation between decodable and accessible
information. Separately, across frozen vision foundation-model backbones,
local intrinsic dimensionality (LID) and within-class spectral decay were
found to track how well such estimators perform. Whether the second explains
the first has not been tested: the reported association is correlational
across backbones, and does not establish that intervening on geometry within
a backbone changes what these estimators recover. We evaluated that
hypothesis on a frozen dermatology foundation model (PanDerm ViT-L/16), with
ISIC 2019 as the in-distribution population and PAD-UFES-20 as the
domain-shifted population; neither dataset formed part of the backbone's
pretraining corpus. Rather than proposing a new detector, we assessed an
explanatory hypothesis under a preregistered protocol in which claims,
statistical tests, and multiplicity correction were fixed before execution.
Representations were reshaped post hoc along an intervention-strength ladder
against a matched-budget fine-tuning control. An implementation-integrity
gate confirmed that domain information and in-distribution task performance
persisted after intervention, and a manipulation check confirmed that the
targeted geometry was measurably altered before any causal comparison was
interpreted. Four estimators---Mahalanobis, cosine-to-centroid,
$k$-nearest-neighbour, and kernel density---were evaluated across five seeds
with Holm--Bonferroni correction. The probe--reliability dissociation
remained statistically detectable but was approximately two orders of
magnitude smaller than originally reported (mean AUROC gap $\approx$0.002
versus $\approx$0.3), with estimators already near ceiling on the unmodified
representation. LID and spectral-decay slope showed no Holm-significant
association with estimator performance; condition number was consistently
associated with all four. A comparison matching geometry across differing
nominal intervention strengths yielded no Holm-significant difference in
degradation. These results provide limited empirical support for a causal
explanatory reading of the geometry--performance association under the
evaluated backbone, layer, and task, and indicate that associations observed
across representations warrant direct intervention-based evaluation before
being interpreted as explanatory.
