## Q1. When AUROC is flat, why? (Check 1)

**Registered predicate** (`reach.check1_reading`, unchanged). Order of tests on (|dAUROC|, S(δ_med), F) at τ=0.002, S_high=0.02, F0=0.0001: (1) inconsistent if |dAUROC| > F beyond sampling error; (2) moved if |dAUROC| ≥ τ; else flat, and then (3) cancelling if F > F0; (4) **invariant** if S ≥ S_high; (5) else **insensitive**. Invariant therefore requires flat AUROC *and* high reach at the observed δ_med *and* almost no realised reordering. It is not a test that δ = 0.

**Why knn10 receives invariant and maha does not (ortho_scale).** Q4 already shows knn10 median |dAUROC| = 0 and maha at 3.0e-06 under ortho_scale — both flat at τ. The label still splits them because invariant demands S(δ_med) ≥ S_high, not δ = 0:

| scorer | n | median_abs_dAUROC | median_delta_med | median_S_med | frac_invariant | frac_insensitive |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| maha | 90 | 3.00e-06 | 2.54 | 7.45e-05 | 0 | 0.744 |
| knn10 | 90 | 0 | 7.29 | 0.999 | 0.989 | 0.0111 |

knn10's *scores* move under scale (median δ_med large) so many ID–OOD pairs sit inside 2δ and S ≈ 1, while ranks — hence AUROC — stay flat: invariant. Mahalanobis is exactly rank-invariant under the affine group (Theorem 1); its ID–OOD gaps dwarf δ_med, so S stays ≪ S_high and the same flat reading is labelled insensitive. Exact rank-invariance with large margins therefore makes the registered invariant label **structurally unreachable**: S(δ) is small precisely because the scorer did not need to move. That is a miscalibration of the label, not a failure of maha. Q3's refit-versus-closed-form relative error of ~1e-5 (float32) and Q4's |dAUROC| ~3e-06 are the arithmetic residual, not an operator effect.

**Claim the paper can support.** Full PanDerm ordering reproduces on 2 of 5 backbones (Kendall τ > 0.8); on the others the mid-ranks permute. What holds on **all five** is that Mahalanobis has by far the lowest reach (S_med 6.1e-04–0.068), with RMD second (0.012–0.072). The ranking of Mahalanobis as least susceptible replicates; the full ordering of the remaining scorers is backbone-dependent.

**Reach saturation is structural, not a failure.** At adapter α=1, S(δ_med) = 1.0 on 80% of (backbone × OOD × seed) cells for knn10/kde/kde_det. Those same scorers move by large |dAUROC| at that dose (Q5): the scores genuinely moved, so a bound on how much they *could* have moved is necessarily uninformative. Reach is large exactly when the response is large, and informative exactly when the response is flat — the only case the audit exists to adjudicate. On this benchmark the flat scorers are Mahalanobis and RMD; their reach stays in a range that still discriminates (see Q5).

The dominant pessimism is λ_min(C): in high-d embeddings the ridge-stabilised smallest eigenvalue is 1e-13–1e-3, so the denominator collapses and the bound inflates by 1/λ_min². "The bound is loose because λ_min(C) is pessimistic by that much," not merely "the bound is loose."

Lemma 2 (cosine, retained-quantity form) holds in 100.0% of 105 configurations at tightness 0.12–0.23 (within an order of magnitude of the observation) — useful, unlike Theorem 2. The kappa-only Lemma 1 form holds in 71.4% and **fails in 28.6%**: it assumes the map acts about the origin, while the audit's maps act about the fit mean. That is the first numerical demonstration that the centred/linear distinction has teeth — worth more than the comparison it was run for.
