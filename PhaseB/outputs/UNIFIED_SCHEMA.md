# Unified run matrix — schema and what it does not license

`unified_run_matrix.csv` joins the per-run matrices of Paper 3 and Paper 4 under
one column vocabulary, so that work building on both has a single table to read.
It is generated by `../analysis/build_unified_matrix.py` from the two sources and
is not edited by hand. Neither source is modified: Paper 3's matrix is published
inside its archived supplement, and rewriting it would put the repository and the
archive out of step.

68 rows — 13 from Paper 3 (one per checkpoint), 55 from Paper 4 (one per
arm/config/seed run, of which 30 are the C2 population).

## Identity

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `study` | `paper3` or `paper4` |
| `unit_id` | checkpoint id, or arm/config/seed |
| `seed` | training seed |
| `condition` | ladder rung, or arm and configuration |
| `dose_kind` | `lambda_orth` (Paper 3) or `alpha` (Paper 4) |
| `dose_value` | intervention strength on that study's own scale |
| `in_primary_population` | whether the row is in the study's analysed population |

`dose_value` is **not** a common scale. Paper 3 varies an orthogonality penalty
over {0, 1, 5}; Paper 4 scales an adapter displacement over [0, 1]. They are
different interventions, and `dose_kind` exists so that nothing pools them by
accident.

## Shared columns

| column | comparable across studies? |
|---|---|
| `geom_condition_number` | name only |
| `ood_{mahalanobis,cosine,knn10}_auroc_directed` | **no** — see below |
| `ood_{mahalanobis,cosine,knn10}_separability` | yes, with care |

**Directed AUROC does not transfer.** Paper 3's Mahalanobis sits at 0.402 under
the higher-distance-is-more-OOD convention. That study is explicit that this is
an inverted ordering, not an absent signal. Paper 4's sits at 0.998. Averaging or
regressing across the two on a `*_auroc_directed` column produces a number with
no meaning.

The `*_separability` columns are `max(a, 1-a)`: the ordering strength with the
direction discarded. They are the defensible cross-study quantity.

| scorer | Paper 3 directed → separability | Paper 4 directed → separability |
|---|---|---|
| Mahalanobis | 0.402 → 0.598 | 0.998 → 0.998 |
| cosine | 0.418 → 0.582 | 0.971 → 0.971 |
| k-NN (k=10) | 0.414 → 0.586 | 0.996 → 0.996 |

**Condition number shares a name, not a scale.** Paper 3's mean is about
1.5 × 10³ on a 16-dimensional representation; Paper 4's is about 1.6 × 10⁶ on a
1024-dimensional frozen backbone. The metric is defined the same way. The
representations are not, so the magnitudes are not on a common footing.

## Study-specific columns

Present in Paper 3 only: `geom_fisher_hl`, `geom_fisher_scalar`,
`geom_mardia_b`, `geom_mardia_z`, `ood_knn1_auroc_directed`,
`ood_knn50_auroc_directed`, `probe_lr_auroc`, `probe_svm_auroc`,
`probe_rf_auroc`.

Present in Paper 4 only: `geom_lid_mean`, `geom_spectral_decay_slope`,
`geom_per_direction_variance_w`, `ood_kde_auroc_directed`,
`probe_domain_accuracy`, `id_task_balanced_accuracy`, `gate0_pass`.

**Probe columns are not two names for one quantity.** Paper 3 reports probe
*AUROC* for three classifier families; Paper 4 reports mean domain-probe
*accuracy*. A probe at 0.735 AUROC and a probe at 0.999 accuracy are not on the
same axis, and the schema keeps them apart rather than inviting the comparison.

**k-NN is only shared at k = 10.** Paper 3 evaluates k ∈ {1, 10, 50}; Paper 4
evaluates k = 10.

## Using it

Filter on `study` for anything within one study. For anything across both, use
`*_separability` rather than `*_auroc_directed`, restrict to
`in_primary_population`, and treat `geom_condition_number` as a same-named
quantity on two different scales rather than as a shared covariate.

Regenerate with:

    python3 PhaseB/analysis/build_unified_matrix.py
