# Architecture Resolution AR-02

**Architecture Resolution, not an Execution Amendment, not a bug fix, not a Protocol Deviation.**
Same category as AR-01: a scientific design question, resolved through evidence, not through
extraction or computation. Traced from the C3 manipulation check
(`outputs/stage5_c3_manipulation_check.json`, 2026-08-06): the β-scaled ℓ2-normalization knob
(`22_Experimental_Design.md` §5) moves condition_number substantially (mean Δ=−273,546) while
leaving LID and spectral-decay slope essentially unchanged (mean Δ≈0 for both, to four decimal
places) — exactly the failure mode `23_Experiment_Contract.md` §4 flagged in Phase A as "the single
most serious technical gap in this design," with a required fix already written: if the knob doesn't
move LID/slope, it needs replacing with a manipulation that acts on those quantities directly.

**Not a setback — the manipulation check did its job.** Per Vinh: this is the reason causal
experiments carry a manipulation check at all. Found before C3 was interpreted, not after — the
alternative (running C3, writing it up, and having a reviewer point out the normalization never
touched LID) would have been far more costly. It also independently confirms the C2/C4 reading
already reached: condition_number responds mainly to scale, LID/slope respond to what Gate 1 was
built to call geometry — the earlier decision not to treat condition_number as sufficient
manipulation evidence looks more correct now, not less.

## The question

*How can LID and within-class spectral-decay geometry be manipulated directly, while preserving as
much of the representation's semantic content as possible?*

A scientific question, not an implementation one. `23_Experiment_Contract.md` §4 named "partial
whitening/eigenvalue reshaping" as **one illustrative example**, not a specified method — picking a
specific mechanism without first surveying alternatives would itself be introducing a new
methodological decision unilaterally, exactly what AR-01's own literature-first correction was built
to avoid repeating.

## Why this is an AR, not an EA or a bug

- **Not an implementation bug**: the code correctly measures what the knob does; the knob itself
  doesn't do what Prediction 2 needs.
- **Not an Execution Amendment**: EA handles a known "what" with a missing "how much/where" (a
  missing artifact, split, embedding). Here, the "what" — which mechanism actually manipulates
  LID/spectral-decay — has never been specified beyond an illustrative example.
- **Not a Protocol Deviation**: `22_Experimental_Design.md` §5 names "β-scaled ℓ2-normalization" as
  the intended technique, but its own hypothesis (Prediction 2) is about LID/spectral-decay, not
  about any specific mechanism for moving them — replacing the mechanism with one that actually
  targets those quantities serves the same preregistered prediction, it doesn't change it.

## Explicitly prohibited at this stage

No choosing whitening, eigenvalue clipping, spectral normalization, or any other specific mechanism
without first surveying what exists. No code. No implementation.

## Resolution path (two steps, mirroring AR-01)

**Step 1 — Literature survey (now open).** Scope: techniques for directly manipulating a
representation's local intrinsic dimensionality and/or spectral (eigenvalue) structure, with
attention to how much semantic/task-relevant content each preserves. Not scoped to any one
candidate family in advance — partial whitening is one possibility to find evidence on, not the
starting assumption. Same discipline as AR-01 Step 1: real citations, verified via fetch, not
recalled from memory; theoretical-saturation stopping rule, not a target count; no proposal for
Paper 4 anywhere in this step.

**Four required evaluation criteria per mechanism (added 2026-08-06, per Vinh)** — not just "does it
move LID/slope," so Step 2 can select on fit-to-design, not just raw efficacy:

1. **Geometry efficacy** — is there evidence it actually changes LID and/or spectral-decay
   structure specifically (not just scale)?
2. **Semantic preservation** — does it hold onto class/task-relevant information, or does it distort
   the representation?
3. **Post-hoc applicability** — can it be applied directly to already-frozen embeddings, or does it
   require retraining?
4. **Dose controllability** — does it have a continuous strength parameter capable of producing a
   ladder (matching the project's own α-ladder pattern), or is it binary/on-off?

**Step 2 — Answer AR-02 (not started until Step 1 exists and is reviewed).** Given the survey,
which mechanism (or minimal combination) manipulates LID/spectral-decay directly while preserving
representation semantics as well as the evidence allows, and fits Paper 4's own constraints (frozen
backbone, applied post hoc to already-adapted embeddings, computationally cheap — CPU-bound per
§8's own resource framing).

## What resolving this well would buy

If AR-02 lands on a real geometry-targeting manipulation, Paper 4 ends up with three structurally
distinct interventions, not two: the Intervention arm (representation intervention), the Conventional
arm (matched-budget control), and the geometry manipulation (C3) — a genuinely tighter causal story
than the current two-intervention design.

## Step 2 answer

**Selected family:** Post-hoc continuous eigenvalue-spectrum reshaping.
**Instantiation:** Fixed-γ Spectral Tempering (Li, Eustratiadis, Kanoulas, arXiv:2603.19339, SIGIR
2026) — `λ_i → λ_i · λ_i^(−γ/2)`, γ a single manually-set scalar swept across a discrete ladder
(γ∈{0,.25,.5,.75,1}), mirroring the project's existing α-ladder design, rather than the paper's own
headline SNR-adaptive γ(k).

**Why fixed-γ is a valid instantiation of the same mechanism, not a deviation from it** (verbatim,
reviewed and amended by Vinh 2026-08-06):

Mechanism and parameter-policy are separable. Spectral Tempering's transform is a power-law
reweighting of the eigenvalue spectrum indexed by dimension. Whether γ is computed adaptively from
local SNR or fixed by hand is a question of how the exponent is *chosen*, not what the transform
*does*. A constant γ is not a different mechanism; it is the degenerate case γ(k)=γ for all k, still
the same functional form, still reshaping the spectrum rather than moving it to a fixed target.

This is grounded in the source paper's own framing, not an ad hoc simplification: it names fixed-γ
spectral scaling as prior, established art — "intermediate spectral scaling methods unify these
extremes by reweighting dimensions with a power coefficient γ, but treat γ as a fixed hyperparameter
that requires task-specific tuning." The paper's contribution, adaptivity, solves one specific
problem: avoiding per-task tuning cost at deployment, where no supervision signal is available to set
γ. That is a deployment-scale retrieval-serving concern, and it does not describe Paper 4's setting.

Paper 4 is not deploying at scale without supervision — it runs a small number of controlled, offline
conditions, and it already has a preregistered, independent criterion for whether a given γ succeeded:
Gate 1 (EA-03's locked wording — reproducible dose dependence in at least one preregistered geometry
metric). Paper 4's protocol provides an independent validation criterion for a pre-specified γ ladder,
rather than an adaptive parameter-selection rule — the ladder (γ∈{0,.25,.5,.75,1}), Gate 1, and the
pass rule are all fixed in advance, not chosen by looking at which γ gives the prettiest result. Where
the source paper needed adaptivity because tuning wasn't available, Paper 4's protocol substitutes a
pre-specified sweep validated against a predefined test, structurally identical to how the α-ladder
already validates dose in the Intervention/Conventional arms.

This argument establishes design validity, not efficacy — whether fixed-γ tempering actually moves
LID/slope in this domain remains an open empirical question, which is why the manipulation check runs
before C3, not instead of it.

## Status

**Locked (2026-08-06).** Selected family: post-hoc continuous eigenvalue-spectrum reshaping.
Instantiation: fixed-γ Spectral Tempering. Not to be reopened except on a demonstrated execution bug
in this AR's own implementation, or on manipulation-check failure — either is an execution outcome of
AR-02, evaluated on its own terms before any decision to reconsider the mechanism family.

Next: (1) implement fixed-γ Spectral Tempering as a post-hoc transform on frozen embeddings, (2) run
its own manipulation check (LID and/or spectral-decay slope must move reproducibly; Gate 0 must still
pass), (3) only if that check passes, rerun C3.
