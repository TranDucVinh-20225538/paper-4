# Confound Map — Paper 4

Assume the hypothesis (`Scientific_Hypothesis.md`) is correct. This lists every factor found this
session that could make us *wrongly conclude* it's correct — i.e., produce the predicted pattern
for a reason that has nothing to do with "geometry mediates accessibility." Ranked by severity.
No methods proposed; controls are named in principle, not specified as protocols.

| # | Confound | Severity | How it biases the conclusion | Control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Embedding dimensionality mismatch** (Paper 3's CNN bottleneck is a deliberately compressed 16-d; a candidate FM's native embedding is typically 512–1024+ d) | **High** | LID, spectral decay, and Mahalanobis behavior are all dimension-sensitive (this is literally Mechanism 4 in `08_Mechanism_Map.md` — distance concentration). Comparing CNN-at-16-d to FM-at-native-dimensionality confounds architecture with dimensionality perfectly; any CNN-vs-FM difference could be a dimensionality artifact, not a geometry-mediation finding. This is probably the single biggest threat to Prediction 3. | Compare at matched dimensionality — e.g., project FM embeddings to 16-d (PCA or a trained bottleneck) as one condition, and/or sweep dimensionality explicitly so architecture and dimensionality are not perfectly collinear. |
| 2 | **Feature normalization convention** (FM embeddings are often used L2-normalized/CLIP-style by community default; the CNN bottleneck features in Papers 1–3 are not) | **High** | Normalization is the *first-order* variable the primary mechanism source (arXiv:2510.15202) manipulates to control Mahalanobis behavior. If each architecture is evaluated under its own "default" convention rather than a shared, explicit scheme, any CNN-vs-FM difference could trivially be a normalization artifact — this directly threatens the "architecture doesn't matter, geometry does" claim, since normalization convention would itself be an architecture-correlated confound. | Evaluate both architectures under the same explicit menu of normalization schemes (raw / L2-normalized / β-scaled), rather than each architecture's community-default convention. |
| 3 | **Foundation-model pretraining data overlap with ISIC/PAD-UFES-20** | **High** (severity conditional — currently unknown whether it applies) | If the candidate FM (PanDerm, MONET, etc.) was pretrained on images from these exact datasets, its "baseline," pre-intervention domain-encoding behavior is not a clean control — it may have already memorized dataset-specific cues no from-scratch CNN would have. This would invalidate any baseline-vs-CNN comparison, not just the intervention effect. | Check each candidate FM's pretraining-corpus documentation for ISIC/PAD-UFES-20 inclusion before backbone selection; if unavoidable, treat as an explicit, disclosed limitation. |
| 4 | **Representation layer / pooling choice for the FM** (CLS token vs. patch-mean vs. intermediate layer) | **High** | Already documented in a different modality (chest radiography, arXiv:2606.11606, logged in `01_Literature_Map.md`) to swing results from chance-level to near-perfect depending on pooling choice alone. Pooling could produce or erase the entire probe-vs-Mahalanobis dissociation being studied, independent of any disentanglement intervention — arguably the most dangerous confound on this list because it has documented precedent for exactly this magnitude of effect. | Report results across at least two pooling/layer choices (e.g., CLS token and patch-mean) rather than committing to one by default. |
| 5 | **Multiple-comparisons / researcher-degrees-of-freedom risk in geometry-metric selection** | **Medium-High** | The geometry toolkit now spans condition number (Paper 3), LID, spectral-decay slope, per-direction variance, and between-domain mean separation (flagged in `10_Prediction_Audit.md`), against 8 reliability scorers. With this many candidate metrics and scorers, *some* correlate of Mahalanobis degradation is likely to turn up by chance even if no real mediation exists — classic exploratory-analysis inflation. | Pre-register which geometry metrics are primary/confirmatory (following Paper 3's own precedent of pre-committing to condition number) before looking at results on any new data; correct for multiple comparisons on the rest. |
| 6 | **ISIC vs. PAD-UFES-20 sample-size imbalance** (~25k vs. ~2.3k images) | **High** | Covariance estimation (which Mahalanobis depends on directly) is far less stable with ~2.3k samples than probe training is — logistic-regression probes are comparatively sample-efficient. Part of the probe-succeeds/Mahalanobis-fails gap could be a covariance-estimation stability artifact of the imbalance itself, present regardless of any geometric mediation story, and present in both the CNN and FM arms identically (so it wouldn't even show up as an architecture effect — it would masquerade as support for architecture-independence for the wrong reason). | Report results under subsampled/balanced covariance estimation as a robustness arm; sweep the shrinkage strength (Paper 2 already uses Ledoit–Wolf shrinkage — extend that sensitivity check here). |
| 7 | **Covariance estimator choice (shrinkage strength, estimator family)** | **Medium** | Distinct from #6 (sample size) but related: even at fixed sample size, different shrinkage/regularization choices for the covariance estimate can change Mahalanobis performance substantially — this is closer to an implementation detail than a "true geometry" effect, but could be mistaken for one if not checked. | Report Mahalanobis results across a shrinkage-strength sweep, as a named robustness check rather than a single fixed choice. |
| 8 | **LID estimator sensitivity** (choice of k in k-NN-based LID estimation, sample size, distance metric) | **Medium** | LID is itself an estimated quantity, sensitive to the neighborhood-size parameter and local sample density. If "LID predicts Mahalanobis degradation" only holds for one specific LID estimator/parameter setting, Mechanism A's reliability as an explanation is weaker than it appears. | Report LID under at least two estimators or parameter settings as a sensitivity check before treating any LID-based finding as robust. |
| 9 | **Probe capacity and regularization** (a "linear probe" is not one fixed procedure — regularization strength, optimizer, epochs all vary) | **Medium** | Papers 1 and 3 already mitigate this somewhat with 5-seed multi-seed probing and shuffle-label sanity checks — but if Paper 4 relaxes or changes this protocol for the FM arm (e.g., different regularization defaults for higher-dimensional FM embeddings), probe-accuracy differences between CNN and FM could be a probe-tuning artifact rather than a true representation difference. | Reuse Paper 1's existing multi-seed + shuffle-label protocol unchanged across both architectures; sweep regularization strength explicitly if embedding dimensionality differs. |
| 10 | **Residual acquisition/clinical-content confounds in the ISIC-vs-PAD-UFES-20 split itself** (inherited from Papers 1–3, not new to Paper 4) | **Medium** | The "domain" label may correlate with more than acquisition modality alone (lesion-type distribution, resolution, color calibration). The probe may be partly recovering these confounds rather than "acquisition shortcut" cleanly — a pre-existing issue in the testbed, now inherited by a new architecture, not created by Paper 4. | Same shuffle-label / majority-baseline sanity checks Papers 1–3 already use, re-applied to the FM setting; no new control needed beyond consistency with prior work. |

---

## Ranked summary

**Highest-severity cluster (must be addressed before any CNN-vs-FM claim is trustworthy):**
dimensionality mismatch (#1), normalization convention (#2), pretraining-data leakage (#3),
pooling/layer choice (#4), sample-size imbalance (#6). Notably, four of these five are specific
to the *architecture comparison* itself, not to the disentanglement intervention — meaning the
single riskiest part of this project is not "does the mechanism explain Paper 3" but "is any
CNN-vs-FM comparison actually apples-to-apples." That risk exists independently of whether the
geometry hypothesis is true.

**Medium cluster:** multiple-comparisons risk (#5), covariance-estimator choice (#7), LID
estimator sensitivity (#8), probe capacity/regularization (#9), inherited acquisition confounds
(#10) — each addressable with a robustness-sweep or a reused existing protocol, none requiring a
new experimental paradigm.

This map does not change the hypothesis. It changes what "confirming" it would have to survive.
